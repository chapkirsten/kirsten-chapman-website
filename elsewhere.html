23.09.26 Chapman on Chapman THINGS I CARE ABOUT TOO MUCH KEEP READING ↓

Ayoade is my role model. Is that healthy?

I watched IT Crowd for the first time in high school and it made something inside of me fundamentally break (that feels negative… let’s go with “shift”). How do I know that something went a little haywire? Well, Richard Ayoade became my role-model. As a 16 year old girl, was I setting my sights on the wrong idol? Perhaps. Did I care then? Nope. Do I care now? Not particularly.

Ayoade goes through life with a form of indifference, scratch that, more like a form of annoyance, which I find commendable. Regardless of the situation, he appears to remain detached and aloof (boy, do I love that word). When you have those things, avoiding hurt seems easier.

But maybe my life-navigating inspirations are a bit psychologically harmful. Instead of aiming for aloofness, we should be aiming for… something else? I don’t know. I’m not a psychologist. Or a philosopher. Or, most certainly, a comedian.

Still, there is something enviable about being able to meet those hard times with a sense of annoyance. It feels counter-culture, and kind of chic, to be able to say “well I don’t care anyway”.

This is an irritating realization, because I do care. A little too deeply about most things. And I think that the more that I try not to care, the more I become a loser who is trying to hide my great capacity for caring under a level of attempted indifference (which, heaven knows, no one buys).

Richard Ayoade would probably have something funny to say about it.

Or he’d just scoff at me.

23.09.26 EXISTENTIAL IRELAND. THINGS I CARE ABOUT TOO MUCH KEEP READING ↓

Irish history has, unfortunately, led me to question free will.

I have a new special interest in my (apparent) quest for geopolitical knowledge… move aside, the Sahel, because Ireland is now in town.

Is this at least partially imbued by my love of “I Love You” by Fontaines D.C.? Perhaps. I can neither confirm nor deny. Primarily because I don’t want to admit to vapidly rooting my intellectual interests in my song of the week.

Anyway, my latest descent into Irish history has brought me to the concept of self-determination.

A dangerous concept to introduce to someone already predisposed to wondering whether she is making choices or simply watching herself make them.

At its broadest, self-determination is the idea that peoples should be able to determine their own political status and pursue their own futures. Reasonable. I would also like to do this.

Unfortunately, I cannot even determine what I will want for lunch tomorrow... Or tbh even today.

Which raises a much less geopolitically consequential question: how much of our own fate can we actually determine?

There are the obvious constraints. Where you’re born. When you’re born. Who raises you. The body you get. The opportunities that happen to exist within driving distance. Whether someone happens to sit next to you in a class one Tuesday and, through a series of increasingly improbable events, permanently alters the trajectory of your life.

And yet we are expected to emerge from all of this and announce, confidently, I chose this.

Did I?

22.09.26 MINOR TRAGEDY. THINGS I CARE ABOUT TOO MUCH KEEP READING ↓

On the particular humiliation of wanting something too much and then not getting it.

There is a particular kind of disappointment that makes you want to die, although not in any practical sense. More in the Victorian sense. You’d like to develop a mysterious fever and be carried upstairs, where everyone is forced to speak in hushed tones about how you had such promise.

This usually happens when you’ve wanted something too much.

You did all the embarrassing things. You imagined getting it. You planned what you’d say when people congratulated you. Perhaps, in an especially humiliating moment, you pictured yourself pretending to be surprised. Then it doesn’t happen and suddenly you’re expected to continue participating in society as though you haven’t just been personally rejected by the universe.

People will tell you that everything happens for a reason. These people should be avoided.

The worst part is that, eventually, you will be fine. You know this because you have been disappointed before and, annoyingly, survived every time. One day this will become a funny anecdote or, worse, character development.

But today it is a tragedy.

Today I will be lying face-down on the bed, fully dressed, listening to something unnecessarily devastating and declining to gain perspective.

Perspective can come tomorrow.

18.09.26 TRYING NOT TO TRY. MARGIN NOTES KEEP READING ↓

The Tao Te Ching has been quietly rearranging some furniture in my brain.

I've been reading the Tao Te Ching lately, which is funny because a substantial portion of my personality is built around trying very hard at things.

The idea I keep coming back to is wu wei — roughly, acting without forcing. Not doing nothing, exactly, but not constantly trying to wrestle everything into the shape you think it should take either.

I am, historically, more of a wrestler.

I don't know that I've become particularly Taoist as a result of reading it. I still make plans. I still care too much about things. I still have approximately seventeen tabs open in my brain at any given moment. But I've found myself noticing when I'm pushing against something simply because I can't tolerate leaving it alone.

Which is mildly irritating, because sometimes an ancient Chinese philosopher really does have a point.

08.09.26 ACRYLIC. MATERIAL CONCERNS KEEP READING ↓

I’ve become a snob. A yarn snob.

I’ve become a snob. A yarn snob. Never once did I think I would become so offended by wound-up plastic. But dear lord, am I revolted. If you had asked me a year ago about acrylic, I would have asked if you were talking about nails. Oh, was I wrong.

It’s a fabric that smothers you, reduces your ability to breathe. Trapping sweat in and making you feel as hot as if you were in the Sahel (still a special interest of mine since the last edition of my rambles).

Acrylic poses a silly thought piece though… when does caring about the material turn you into a soul-deprived goblin? Is it okay to care about yarn? To value a natural product? Or have I become the exact sort of person I would have found deeply annoying a year ago?

Because there is something sinister about acquiring taste. You start innocently enough. You learn the difference between wool and acrylic. Then you learn about merino. Then alpaca. Then cashmere. Suddenly you’re in a shop touching the sleeve of a perfectly innocent sweater, checking the tag, and putting it back with the quiet disgust of a Victorian woman who has just discovered an unsuitable marriage prospect.

My internal battle over my hatred of acrylic has made me realize something: expertise ruins your ability to enjoy things indiscriminately. Once you know what “good” yarn feels like, you can’t unknow it. Is knowing more actually making your life better, or have you simply acquired another category of things to be irritated by?

Perhaps caring about things is nice. Knowing why you like something is nice. Having opinions is, generally, what keeps us from becoming terribly boring people. Maybe the problem isn’t becoming a snob. Maybe it’s becoming an asshole about it.

It’s when your opinions about sheep hair begin informing your opinions about other human beings that perhaps it’s time to reassess.

So yes, I am a yarn snob. I want wool. I want alpaca. I want fibers that once belonged to something with a pulse.

But I will try very hard not to become spiritually ugly about it.

Unless you make me knit with 100% acrylic.

Then all bets are off.
