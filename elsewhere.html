01

MARIANNE.

Fontaines D.C., repeatedly and without moderation.

There is a particular stage of liking a song where listening to it stops being a choice. I have reached that stage with Marianne.

Fontaines D.C. are already dangerously overrepresented in my listening habits, so this isn't exactly a shocking development. But Marianne has lodged itself somewhere particularly inconvenient in my brain. It's beautiful and vaguely devastating and has exactly the kind of atmosphere that makes me want to stare dramatically out of a car window.

Music is probably the thing I could talk about longest with absolutely no invitation to do so. Fontaines D.C., Arctic Monkeys, Jeff Buckley, Kendrick Lamar, Bleech 9:3... the list gets embarrassing quickly. This is therefore less a music recommendation and more a public record of whatever has commandeered my headphones lately.

At the moment: Marianne. Again.

02

SIX HOURS FOR DUMPLINGS.

This is not a restaurant review. It is evidence submitted against me.

This past weekend, I drove six hours to eat Din Tai Fung.

I would like to pretend there were mitigating circumstances. There were not enough of them.

I love Din Tai Fung with the kind of enthusiasm that makes reasonable people start asking questions about opportunity cost. There are closer restaurants. There are restaurants that do not require crossing state lines. And yet.

The problem is that once I've decided I want something specific, “but it is six hours away” apparently enters my brain as logistical information rather than an argument against doing it.

I would also like the record to show that my restaurant opinions contain absolutely no internal consistency. I can be extremely annoying about where I want to eat and then turn around and tell you, with complete sincerity, that I fucking love Olive Garden.

Both things are true. I contain multitudes. Some of them want xiao long bao. Some of them want unlimited breadsticks.

would do it again, unfortunately

03

TRYING NOT TO TRY.

The Tao Te Ching has been quietly rearranging some furniture in my brain.

I've been reading the Tao Te Ching lately, which is funny because a substantial portion of my personality is built around trying very hard at things.

The idea I keep coming back to is wu wei— roughly, acting without forcing. Not doing nothing, exactly, but not constantly trying to wrestle everything into the shape you think it should take either.

I am, historically, more of a wrestler.

I don't know that I've become particularly Taoist as a result of reading it. I still make plans. I still care too much about things. I still have approximately seventeen tabs open in my brain at any given moment. But I've found myself noticing when I'm pushing against something simply because I can't tolerate leaving it alone.

Which is mildly irritating, because sometimes an ancient Chinese philosopher really does have a point.

THE BAG SITUATION.

Songmont Hobo Bag. We are currently in negotiations.

I like vintage clothes because finding something good feels approximately 40% like shopping and 60% like winning a small competition no one else knew they were participating in.

This has not prevented me from wanting new things.

Current problem: the Songmont Hobo Bag.

My general wardrobe philosophy exists somewhere between vintage, Reformation, and convincing myself that one extremely good accessory would suddenly make every outfit I own make sense. I recognize that handbags have historically benefited enormously from this line of reasoning.

I haven't decided whether this particular argument will succeed.

It is going very well for the bag.

ADULTS IS VERY GOOD.

Current television recommendation, filed with enthusiasm.

I've been watching Adults, and it has very quickly entered the category of shows I want other people to watch mostly so I can talk to them about it.

This is generally how I consume things. I don't have a particularly dignified relationship with media I like. A book, show, album, or documentary either passes through my life normally or becomes something I suddenly need to bring up in unrelated conversations.

At the moment, Adults is in the second category.

Also somewhere in the current cultural pile: anything Richard Osman writes, and an increasingly deep rabbit hole of documentaries about the Sahel. There is no coherent theme here. That's probably the point.
